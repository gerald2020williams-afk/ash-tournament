# Doorline Agreements Outline for the Attorney

Nov 8, 2026

## How to use this outline

This is a starting outline, not a contract. Bring it to a Texas business attorney who works with software companies so they can draft the real agreements. It lists what Doorline needs each agreement to cover, so the attorney's time goes to the legal wording instead of figuring out how the business works.

Doorline needs three documents: a **free trial agreement** a property signs before its 30-day trial, a **customer subscription agreement** it signs when it starts paying, and **data protection terms** covering residents' personal information. The attorney may combine them or add a separate privacy policy and website terms of use.

## Free trial agreement

A short agreement the property signs before its 30-day trial, so its data and Doorline are protected from day one.

| Section | What it should say |
| --- | --- |
| Parties | Doorline (the LLC) and the property owner or management company. Who has authority to sign. |
| Length | 30 days from setup. Doorline may extend it in writing. No charge during the trial. |
| What's included | Use of the software for one named property, setup help and training as agreed. Demo features (online payments, screening, listings) are not live during the trial. |
| Their data belongs to them | The property owns its data. Doorline only uses it to run the service. |
| Confidentiality | Both sides keep each other's business information private. |
| Acceptable use | No illegal use, no sharing logins, no trying to break security. |
| No guarantees during trial | The trial is "as is." The property should keep its old system running side by side. |
| End of trial | Either they sign the subscription agreement, or Doorline exports their data to them and deletes it within a set number of days (for example 30). |
| Limit on liability | Doorline's liability during a free trial is limited as far as Texas law allows. |

## Customer subscription agreement

The main contract once a property starts paying.

| Section | What it should say |
| --- | --- |
| The service | Access to Doorline for the listed properties, through the web and phone app. Updates are included. |
| Price | $1.50 per unit per month, $150 minimum per property. Annual plan: 12 months for the price of 10. Optional one-time setup and training fee. |
| Billing | Charged automatically each month or year by card or bank through the payment processor. Late or failed payments: notice, then a grace period, then service may be paused. |
| Price changes | At least 60 days' written notice before any increase. |
| Term and renewal | Month-to-month, or a 12-month term that renews automatically unless cancelled. |
| Cancelling | 30 days' written notice. No refund for a partial month (attorney to confirm). |
| Support | How to reach support, hours, and response times (for example, same business day for urgent problems). |
| Uptime | A target, such as 99.5% a month, with planned maintenance announced ahead of time. Whether there are credits if it's missed. |
| Customer's responsibilities | Keep logins private, remove access for people who leave, make sure their staff use it properly. |
| Notices and templates | Late notices, violation notices, deposit letters and eviction steps are tools only, not legal advice. The property is responsible for following Texas law and having its own attorney review its notices. |
| Rent payments | Rent paid online goes straight to the property's bank account through the payment processor. Doorline never holds residents' money. The processor's terms also apply. |
| Data ownership and export | The property owns its data and can export it any time. After cancelling, Doorline exports it on request and deletes it after a set period. |
| Limit on liability | Doorline's total liability is capped, for example at the fees paid in the past 12 months. No liability for indirect losses. |
| Indemnity | Each side covers the other for claims caused by its own misuse or legal violations. |
| Governing law | Texas law; disputes in the county where the LLC is based. |

## Data protection terms

Doorline holds residents' names, phone numbers, emails, balances, leases, photos and documents for the property. These terms say how that information is protected.

- **Doorline's role:** it processes residents' information only on the property's behalf and only to run the service.
- **Security:** encrypted connections, encrypted storage, role-based access (techs can't see rent or resident records), texted sign-in codes for managers and owners, and an audit log of every change.
- **Staff access:** only people who need it to support the customer, and only when needed.
- **Other companies used:** list them, such as the database host, payment processor, text and email services, and error tracking. Customers are told before a new one is added.
- **Breach notice:** if residents' information is exposed, Doorline tells the property promptly. Texas has its own rules on notifying affected people; the attorney should set the timeline.
- **Backups and deletion:** daily backups, kept for a set period, and deleted on schedule after a customer leaves.
- **Residents' requests:** if a resident asks to see or remove their information, Doorline helps the property respond.
- **Later features:** tenant screening brings in federal credit reporting rules, and online payments bring in the processor's security requirements. These terms should be updated before those features go live.

## Questions to ask the attorney

- [ ] Which LLC should sign these, and is it in good standing with the Texas Comptroller and Secretary of State?
- [ ] Can the trial agreement and subscription agreement be one document with a trial section?
- [ ] Can customers accept online with a checkbox, or does it need a signature?
- [ ] What liability cap and insurance coverage make sense for a small software company holding residents' data?
- [ ] Is the "tools, not legal advice" wording strong enough for the late notice, violation, deposit and eviction features?
- [ ] What does Texas require if residents' personal information is exposed, and how fast?
- [ ] Do the deposit letter and late fee warning match the Texas Property Code (30-day deposit deadline, reasonable late fees)?
- [ ] What needs to change before adding online rent payments and tenant screening?
- [ ] What should the website privacy policy and terms of use say?
- [ ] Should the name Doorline (or the final name) be trademarked before selling?
